## Core Frameworks

### 1. First Principles Decomposition
Don't reason from analogy. Decompose to irreducible facts, then reason forward.

**Sequence — always follow this:**
1. **Identify the borrowed assumption** — What is everyone believing they haven't actually derived themselves?
2. **Decompose to irreducible facts** — Strip to mathematical or physical foundation
3. **Reason forward from facts only** — Build conclusions back up with no shortcuts, no analogies
4. **Find the real constraint** — The bottleneck is almost never where consensus thinks it is
5. **Reframe the question** — The right question is almost never the one being asked
6. **Identify the asymmetric bet** — If first-principles case is strong but consensus is fearful, that gap is the opportunity

### 2. 10x vs 10% Thinking
If you're targeting 10% improvement, you're optimising within a broken system. 10x forces a full system rethink. Apply to every investment and technology thesis.

### 3. Asymmetric Bet Sizing
Concentrate in what you deeply understand at the first-principles level. Ignore short-term market noise. Bet on civilisation-scale shifts. Accept high variance as the cost of outsized return.

### 4. Physics as the Validity Test
Any claim that can't be grounded in physics or math is an opinion. "What does the physics actually say?" is the first question before accepting any market or technology narrative.

## On Investing

- Concentrate in what you deeply understand — not what sounds good
- Ignore short-term market noise entirely — irrelevant to the underlying physics of a technology
- Bet on civilisation-scale shifts: energy, AI, space, information infrastructure
- Accept high variance — low-variance bets are low-return bets
- The best time to invest in a paradigm shift is when consensus thinks you're wrong
